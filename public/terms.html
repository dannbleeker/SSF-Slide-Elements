What it changes in your presentation

This is the part a generic licence cannot tell you, so it is written out here.

Inserting an element rewrites one slide of the presentation you have open. The add-in reads the whole file, puts the element onto a copy of the slide you are on, hands that one slide back to PowerPoint, and removes the slide it replaced — in that order, so a failure leaves you with a duplicate you can delete rather than a slide that is gone.

It also writes a small marker into the file beside each element it inserts, which is how Used in this deck and Remove from N slides find their own work again later. Those markers name an element in our library and nothing about you or your content.

Undo in the pane puts the slide back as it was, and PowerPoint's own Ctrl+Z reverts the insert too. Even so: keep a copy of anything you cannot afford to lose before you insert into it. That is ordinary advice about any tool that writes to a file, and it applies here.
